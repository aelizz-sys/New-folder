Three Days on the Ridge Trail

A beginner-friendly backpacking trip with pine forests, alpine lakes, and the best sunsets in the state.

By Priya Shah | October 2, 2026 | 6 min read

Why This Trail?

The Ridge Trail is a 22-mile loop that climbs through pine forest, crosses two open ridgelines, and ends beside a string of quiet alpine lakes. It is long enough to feel like a real adventure, but gentle enough for first-time backpackers.

Most hikers finish the loop in three days. Campsites are spaced about seven miles apart, water is easy to find, and the trail is marked with bright orange blazes, so getting lost is almost impossible.

"By the second night, I stopped checking my phone. I just watched the sky."

The real reward is the light. Around 7 p.m. the sun drops behind the western peaks and the whole valley turns gold, then pink, then deep blue. Pack a warm layer and plan to sit still for a while.

Permits are free but required. Pick one up at the ranger station on the morning you start.

The 3-Day Plan

Each day covers 7 to 8 miles, with plenty of time for breaks, snacks, and photos.

Day 1: Trailhead to Pine Camp

  • 7 miles
  • 1,400 ft climb
  • About 4 hours

Start early to beat the afternoon heat. The first two miles follow a creek through tall pines, with a few wooden bridges and plenty of shade.

After the creek, the trail switchbacks up to Pine Camp, a flat clearing with space for about ten tents. Set up camp, filter water from the spring, and rest your legs.

If you still have energy, take the short side path to Lookout Rock. It adds half a mile and gives you your first view of the ridge you will cross tomorrow.

Day 2: Pine Camp to Mirror Lake

  • 8 miles
  • 900 ft climb
  • About 5 hours

Today is the highlight. You leave the forest behind and walk the open ridgeline for almost three miles, with views in every direction.

Wind can be strong up here, so keep your jacket handy and your hat clipped on. The trail drops gently to Mirror Lake by mid-afternoon.

Mirror Lake is perfectly still most evenings. Grab a campsite on the north shore for the best sunset reflections.

Day 3: Mirror Lake to Trailhead

  • 7 miles
  • 1,300 ft descent
  • About 3.5 hours

The last day is mostly downhill through aspen groves. Take it slow on the steeper sections, because tired legs make small mistakes.

About a mile from the end, the trail rejoins the creek from Day 1, so the loop closes right where it began.

Back at the trailhead, the general store sells cold drinks and the best cinnamon rolls you will ever eat after three days of trail food.
